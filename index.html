How compression works

Lower quality usually means smaller files. PNG is lossless and ignores this slider. Re-encoding an already compressed image can make it larger; compare the result size.

The actual output bytes, including any metadata you keep, are checked against your limit (1 KB = 1,024 bytes). Quality never exceeds the slider. If the target cannot be met, the app reports an error instead of downloading an oversized file.

Privacy

On by default. Uncheck to keep supported metadata.

Metadata can reveal GPS locations, dates, and names. Old embedded previews are still removed. Some fields cannot be kept when converting formats.

AI can miss faces. Review every image and adjust covers in the face editor.

What privacy tools remove

With removal on, original EXIF, GPS, IPTC, XMP, comments, and embedded thumbnails are not copied. Your original files stay unchanged.

With removal off, supported EXIF, GPS, IPTC, XMP, and comments are copied back. Image size and orientation reflect the processed pixels. Old previews, opaque camera MakerNotes, and original color profiles are not copied. The metadata engine loads locally on first use.

WebP keeps EXIF and XMP, but not native IPTC or image comments. PNG stores IPTC in a legacy text block that some apps may not read.

AI detects faces locally. Small, obscured, or side-on faces may be missed. Add or adjust solid covers in the face editor. Existing covers stay until you remove them.

Without neutral filenames, original names are retained with a suffix. Visible addresses, text, reflections, and other identifying details are not automatically removed.
